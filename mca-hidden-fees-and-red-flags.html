MCA Hidden Fees and Contract Red Flags

Updated November 18, 2026 · 3 min read

The factor rate is only part of what an MCA can cost, and the contract contains terms that affect your risk as much as the price. Here are the fees and clauses worth looking for, and the warning signs that call for a second look.

Fees that raise your real cost

  • Origination or underwriting fees: deducted from the advance, so you receive less than the headline amount while still repaying the full payback.
  • Administrative or processing fees: sometimes charged per debit or as a flat amount.
  • Broker commissions: if a broker arranges the deal, their commission may be built into the factor rate or added as a fee.
  • UCC filing fees: charged for registering the funder's claim on your business assets or receivables.
  • Penalties and default fees: for missed or returned payments.

Our calculator has a field for percentage-based upfront fees. Use it, because fees paid out of the advance can lift the estimated APR significantly.

Contract terms to understand

  • Personal guarantee: may make you personally responsible if the business cannot pay.
  • Blanket lien or UCC filing: may give the funder a claim on business assets, not just receivables.
  • Reconciliation clause: explains whether payments can be adjusted if your sales drop. Ask how and how fast.
  • Default triggers: some contracts treat things beyond missed payments, such as changing bank accounts, as a default.
  • Stacking restrictions: limits on taking additional financing while the advance is outstanding.
  • Confession of judgment: a clause allowing a quick court judgment against you. Some states restrict or prohibit it, so ask a professional what applies where you operate.
  • Prepayment terms: whether paying early earns any discount at all.

Red flags in the sales process

  • Pressure to sign today or "this offer expires in an hour."
  • Unwillingness to put the total payback, payment amount and net funding in writing.
  • No clear answer to "what is the estimated APR?"
  • A request for a large upfront payment before funding.
  • Vague answers about who the actual funder is, or who is being paid.
Check your state's rules. A number of states have commercial financing disclosure laws that can require providers to show certain costs, including an estimated APR. Rules differ, so confirm what applies to you.

What to do next

Read the whole agreement, not just the summary. Run the numbers in the calculator, and use our checklist of questions in your conversation with the funder. For a large or unusual deal, consider having an attorney or a qualified advisor review it.
