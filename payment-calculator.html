How this calculator works

The total payback is the funding amount multiplied by the factor rate. We spread it evenly over the number of payments in the term (22 business days per month for daily payments, or about 4.33 weeks per month for weekly ones) to get your payment. Your monthly repayments are the total payback divided by the number of months.

The share of revenue is monthly repayments divided by your average monthly revenue. If you add a gross margin, we also show the share of gross profit, which is the money left after the direct costs of making your product or delivering your service. Revenue still has to pay for materials, payroll and rent, so gross profit is often the more realistic yardstick.

How to read the result

  • Test a slow month. Enter your weakest recent monthly revenue instead of the average and see whether the payment still fits.
  • Count your other commitments. Rent, payroll, taxes and existing loans come out of the same bank account.
  • Remember fixed payments do not flex. A fixed daily debit is taken even when sales are low, unless your contract adjusts payments to sales.

Frequently asked questions

What share of revenue should MCA payments take?

There is no universal answer. It depends on your margins, fixed costs and how steady your sales are. Many owners test the payment against their weakest recent month rather than the average, and compare it with gross profit rather than revenue, since revenue still has to cover costs such as inventory and payroll.

What if my MCA takes a percentage of daily sales?

Then your payment moves with your sales. The share of revenue stays roughly equal to the holdback percentage in your contract, but the time it takes to repay changes. This calculator assumes equal fixed payments, so treat it as an estimate and check the holdback percentage in your agreement.

Why does the calculator show monthly figures for a daily payment?

Revenue and costs are usually tracked monthly, so converting daily or weekly payments into a monthly amount makes them easy to compare with your income. We divide the total payback by the number of months in the term.

Does this include fees?

The fee field takes a percentage of the funding amount out of the money you receive. It does not change your payment, but it raises the estimated APR because you get less cash for the same payback.
