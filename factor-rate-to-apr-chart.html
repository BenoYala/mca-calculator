Factor rate to APR chart

Last updated November 18, 2026

A factor rate tells you how much you repay, but not how expensive the money is over time. This chart converts common factor rates into an estimated APR for different terms, so you can see at a glance how the same factor rate changes with how fast you repay.

How to read the chart

  • Find your factor rate in the left column and your repayment term across the top.
  • Read the estimated APR where they meet. For example, a 1.3 factor rate over 6 months is about 104% APR.
  • Notice the pattern. Moving down a row (higher factor) raises the cost, and moving right (longer term) lowers the APR even though the dollar cost stays the same.

Why the same factor rate gives different APRs

The factor rate sets a fixed cost. The APR expresses that cost per year on the money you actually hold, and you repay a little every day, so a shorter term means a higher annual rate. That is why comparing factor rates alone can mislead: 1.2 over 3 months can be more expensive per year than 1.4 over 12 months.

What this chart does not include

  • Upfront fees, broker commissions and other charges, which raise the real APR.
  • Percentage-of-sales repayment, early-payoff discounts and renewals.
  • Whether you would qualify for these terms.
