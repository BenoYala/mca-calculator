How this calculator works

The cost of the advance is the total payback (funding amount times the factor rate) minus the cash you actually receive after fees. The calculator compares that cost with the extra profit you expect from using the money.

Cost of advance      = Payback − Cash received
Profit during term   = Extra profit per month × (Term − Delay)
Net result           = Profit during term − Cost of advance
Cost covered after   = Delay + Cost ÷ Extra profit per month

What counts as extra profit

Enter only the additional profit that exists because you used the money: for example the margin on inventory you could not otherwise buy, or the revenue from an extra crew minus what that crew costs. Do not include sales you would have made anyway. If you are unsure, use a lower estimate, since results are very sensitive to this one number.

Limits of the model

  • It assumes the money itself is recovered through the business use (for example stock is sold). If the money is spent on something that does not return, the real cost to you is higher.
  • Payments start right away, while profit can arrive later. Make sure you can fund payments during the delay.
  • Profit after the term ends is not counted, so a result below zero can still turn positive later.

Frequently asked questions

What is a payback period?

A payback period is how long it takes for an investment to earn back what it cost. Here it is the time until the extra profit from using the money covers the cost of the advance.

Why compare profit with the cost, not the full repayment?

The model assumes the money itself comes back through its business use, for example inventory is sold for more than it cost. So only the cost of the advance has to be covered by extra profit. If the money does not come back that way, enter a lower profit figure or treat the full repayment as the amount to cover.

Does it account for the timing of cash?

Only in a simple way. The delay field removes the first months from the profit. Because payments start right away, you should also check that your cash flow can handle the payments while you wait for profit to arrive.

What if the result is negative?

It means that under your assumptions the extra profit during the term does not cover the advance's cost. You could test a longer term, a lower factor rate, or a different way to use the money, or consider cheaper financing.
