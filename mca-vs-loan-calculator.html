How the comparison works

The advance is priced with a factor rate: total payback is the funding amount multiplied by the factor, repaid in equal daily or weekly payments. The loan is priced with a rate and repaid in equal monthly payments, using the standard amortization formula. Fees reduce the cash you receive for both offers.

To compare them on equal terms we calculate the estimated APR of each the same way: the periodic rate that makes all payments equal to the money you actually receive, annualized. For the loan this means the APR includes any origination fee you enter.

Why the monthly outflow differs so much

An advance is repaid over a short term, so the same amount of money goes out of your account each month much faster than with a longer loan. A loan with a long term has a lower monthly payment but can cost more in interest over time. The monthly outflow row shows the effect on cash flow, and total cost and APR show the effect on price.

What this tool does not compare

  • Approval requirements, speed of funding and paperwork.
  • Collateral, personal guarantees and what happens if you miss payments.
  • Whether you could qualify for the loan at the rate you entered.

Frequently asked questions

Which is cheaper, an advance or a loan?

That depends on the numbers you enter. A loan with a lower APR usually costs less in total, but approval, speed, collateral and your cash flow also matter. This tool compares cost only.

Why is the advance's monthly outflow so much higher?

Because it is repaid over a few months instead of years. The same total payback is squeezed into fewer, larger monthly amounts, which strains cash flow even though the total cost might be similar.

Should I enter an interest rate or an APR for the loan?

Enter whatever the lender quotes. If the number is an APR that already includes fees, leave the origination fee at 0 so the fee is not counted twice. If it is only an interest rate, add the origination fee separately.

Why do the two offers show different amounts received?

Fees are deducted from the money you receive, and you may enter different amounts for each offer. Compare the cost per $1 received to see which gives you cash more cheaply.
