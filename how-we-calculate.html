How we calculate

Last updated November 18, 2026

A calculator is only useful if you can trust it. This page explains exactly how our numbers are produced, what we assume, how much those assumptions can move the answer, and how you can check the math yourself.

Our principles

  • Estimates, clearly labeled. We give you an estimate to compare offers, not a replacement for your contract.
  • Assumptions in plain sight. Every assumption is listed below and on the calculators.
  • Checkable. You can reproduce every result with a spreadsheet.
  • Corrected when wrong. If you find a mistake, tell us and we will fix it and say so.

The APR method

An MCA is quoted with a factor rate, so we first convert it into payments:

Total payback     = Funding amount × Factor rate
Cash received     = Funding amount × (1 − Upfront fee %)
Payments (n)      = Term in months × payments per month
Payment           = Total payback ÷ n

Then we find the periodic rate r that makes the present value of all payments equal to the cash you actually received. This is the same idea behind the APR on a loan:

Cash received = Σ  Payment ÷ (1 + r)^k        k = 1 … n

Estimated APR          = r × payments per year
Effective annual rate  = (1 + r)^(payments per year) − 1

There is no closed-form solution for r, so we solve it numerically. We also show a "flat annualized cost", which is simply cost ÷ cash received ÷ years. That figure ignores the fact that you repay gradually, so it understates the true cost of a daily-payment advance. We show it because some brokers quote it.

Assumptions

  • Payments are equal in size. Real contracts may take a percentage of daily sales instead.
  • Daily payments fall on 22 business days per month (252 per year). Weekly payments use 52 per year.
  • Upfront fees are a percentage of the funding amount, deducted from the cash you receive.
  • There are no other costs, such as broker fees outside the contract, penalties or renewals.
  • The term is the time to pay back the full amount, with no early-payoff discount.

How much do the assumptions matter?

Different months have different numbers of business days, and some funders debit on a different calendar. Here is the example offer ($50,000, factor 1.30, 6 months, daily payments) under different business-day assumptions:

The other calculators

  • Payment calculator: monthly repayments = total payback ÷ term in months. Share of revenue = monthly repayments ÷ monthly revenue. Share of gross profit = monthly repayments ÷ (revenue × gross margin).
  • MCA vs. loan: the loan payment uses the standard amortization formula, P × i ÷ (1 − (1 + i)^−n) with i = annual rate ÷ 12. Its APR is solved the same way as the advance's, on the cash you receive after any origination fee.
  • Payback period: cost of advance = total payback − cash received. Net result = extra profit per month × (term − delay) − cost. Months to cover the cost = delay + cost ÷ extra profit per month.

What we do not model

  • Percentage-of-sales repayment and reconciliation adjustments.
  • Renewals, refinancing, consolidation and early-payoff discounts.
  • Taxes, and the effect of the cost on your tax position.
  • Whether you would qualify for any of the products compared.
