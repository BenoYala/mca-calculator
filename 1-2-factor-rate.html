What APR is a 1.2 factor rate?

Updated November 18, 2026 · Estimates for daily payments, no fees unless stated

Quick answer. A 1.2 factor rate costs $2,000 for every $10,000 advanced. With daily payments and no upfront fees, that is about 75% APR over 6 months and 38% over 12 months. Open this offer in the calculator

What a 1.2 factor rate costs in dollars

A factor rate is a multiplier: you repay the amount advanced times 1.2. That means $1 back for every $1 you receive, so the cost is $2,000 per $10,000 no matter how long you take to repay.

How long a term brings the APR down?

The estimated APR stays at or above 100% for terms up to 4 months and falls below it for longer terms. It stays at or above 36% APR for terms up to 12 months. Stretching a 1.2 factor rate from 3 to 12 months lowers the estimated APR from about 149% to about 38%, yet the total you repay does not change at all.

How upfront fees change the APR

If the funder deducts a fee from the advance, you receive less cash but still repay the full payback, so the true cost rises. Here is a 1.2 factor rate over 6 months with different fees:

Frequently asked questions

What APR is a 1.2 factor rate?

It depends on the term. With daily payments and no fees, a 1.2 factor rate is roughly 149% APR over 3 months, 75% over 6 months and 38% over 12 months. The longer you take to repay, the lower the APR, because the same fixed cost is spread over more time.

How much does a $50,000 advance cost at a 1.2 factor rate?

You repay $60,000 in total, so the advance costs $10,000. If it is repaid over 6 months with daily payments, that is about $454.55 per business day.

Is a 1.2 factor rate good?

There is no universal answer. Factor rates on offers commonly fall between about 1.1 and 1.5, but the term, the fees and how payments are collected matter as much as the number itself. Convert the offer to an estimated APR and compare it with other financing, including bank and SBA loans, before deciding.
